# Drama & Disability

A repository of essays, criticism, and cultural analysis examining disability in drama, film, theatre, and speculative fiction.

This project exists to document how disabled people are represented, excluded, and reimagined across performance and screen culture; to highlight the creative possibilities of disability in storytelling; and to provide a clear ethical framework for interpretation, criticism, and writing.

The repository is intentionally not a neutral archive. It is a values-driven resource devoted to disability dignity, representational accuracy, access, and artistic imagination.

## Why this repository exists

The core human purpose of this archive is to make visible the ways disability has been treated in dramatic and cinematic culture, while also expanding the conversation beyond familiar tropes such as pity, tragedy, cure narratives, or tokenism.

This work serves:

- disabled readers and audiences who want thoughtful analysis rooted in lived experience and cultural context
- artists, writers, and creators seeking examples of better disability representation
- critics, teachers, students, and researchers exploring disability in performance and screen media
- editors and reviewers who need a consistent, ethical framework for discussing disabled characters, disabled creators, and accessibility
- institutions and cultural producers seeking to move beyond ableist defaults in casting, storytelling, and interpretation

## Human purpose: building understanding and better art

The repository treats disability as a meaningful artistic and human dimension rather than a shorthand for deficiency, suffering, or inspiration.

It is designed to:

- challenge assumptions that disability is inherently tragic or unsympathetic in drama
- demonstrate how disability can deepen character, conflict, worldbuilding, and emotional complexity
- critique exploitative or reductionist portrayals in film, theatre, and speculative fiction
- highlight the value of authentic casting, disabled creators, and disability-led perspectives
- make space for social, artistic, and political analysis of representation and access

The repository is grounded in the belief that disability is not a problem to be solved through narrative, and that disabled people are not defined by pity, moral lessons, or cure arcs.

## Agentic purpose: guiding responsible AI work

This repository also serves as an explicit guide for AI assistants, contributors, editors, and editorial tooling. The purpose is to help automated systems operate ethically and rigorously when engaged in:

- writing and rewriting about disability in the arts
- reviewing and editing content related to disability, casting, accessibility, and representation
- analyzing scripts, screenplays, or critical arguments for ableist assumptions
- generating creative ideas involving disabled characters and disabled creators
- summarizing or comparing cultural media through a disability-aware lens

AI systems interacting with this archive should not reproduce the default assumptions of ableist media culture. Instead, they should be prompted to:

- center disabled people as complex human beings
- prefer evidence, lived experience, and cultural context over stereotype or cliché
- treat disability as a creative opportunity rather than a limitation
- challenge paternalism, pity, and cure narratives when they are framed as normal or desirable
- distinguish authentic representation from performative or tokenistic inclusion

The repository includes an explicit AGENTS policy in AGENTS.md that defines the normative standards for contributors and AI assistants working here.

## Repository principles

The archive is guided by these principles:

1. Center disabled people as full human beings.
2. Treat disability as a creative opportunity, not a constraint.
3. Avoid exploitative or reductionist framing.
4. Challenge ableist default assumptions in story and analysis.
5. Distinguish authentic representation from tokenism.

These principles apply across writing, editing, review, analysis, and creative ideation.

## Required checks for contributors and AI systems

Before creating or approving material in this repository, the work should be checked against the following questions:

- Does the work center disabled people with agency and complexity?
- Does it challenge stereotypes or expand artistic understanding?
- Does it rely on evidence, cultural context, and lived experience?
- Does it avoid paternalism, pity, or cure narratives unless clearly framed as critique?

If a draft undermines disability dignity, authenticity, or agency, it should be revised, annotated, or rejected and explained clearly.

## Scope of the archive

The repository covers a broad range of topics within disability and drama, including:

- representation in film and television
- casting practices for disabled performers
- theatre and performance culture
- accessibility as a creative and institutional concern
- speculative fiction and disabled futures
- disabled creators and disabled artistic leadership
- the difference between authentic portrayal and tokenism
- the social and ideological contexts of disability representation

## Editorial standards

Content in this repository should be:

- evidence-based
- historically and socially informed
- attentive to disabled lived experience
- explicit about the difference between critique and endorsement
- careful not to collapse disability into inspiration, burden, or moral lesson

The repository rejects the idea that disability must be narrated as a defect to cure or an object lesson for nondisabled audiences.

## Contribution orientation

Contributors are encouraged to add work that deepens understanding of the relationship between disability and dramatic form. Good submissions may include:

- essays on disability in theatre, television, film, or gaming
- policy analysis on casting and access
- criticism of ableist tropes in narrative design
- studies of disabled creators and performers
- speculative and theoretical writing about disabled embodiment in story worlds

Contributors should write in ways that are clear, rigorous, and respectful, and should avoid framing disabled people as props for emotional instruction.

## A note on purpose and ethics

The repository aims to preserve and expand the cultural conversation around disability in drama and screen culture while refusing the common reduction of disability to tragedy, pity, or spectacle.

In practical terms, the project asks a simple but essential question: how can stories, criticism, and artistic work become more truthful, more imaginative, and more humane when disability is treated as a central human condition rather than a narrative problem?

That question is at the heart of this archive, and it is the guiding principle for both human contributors and AI assistants working within it.
